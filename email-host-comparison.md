# Personal Email Host Comparison

**Before I start, I would like to emphasise that I am not sponsored by Mailbox.org, this will be my honest opinion**

 | Provider | Price/Month | (Important) Features | Why/Why not |
| ------------- | ------------- | ------------- | ------------- |
| Mailbox.org  | €3 | 20GB Mail Storage, Can use Custom Domain Names, 50 aliases for custom domain names, OpenPGP integration | Fair price/value consideration while balancing privacy needs too. It is also fully independent from large US providers (better not to put all your eggs in one basket), and importantly, **we know that the servers are in Germany, and hosted by them** so *where* our data is not as big of a question. However, they only have a progressive web app on mobile and *no* support for scheduled send on said app, requiring you to use an external app - and as far as I am aware, I am yet to find an app that supports *both* sending from aliases and scheduled email sending. |
| Proton | £3.99 (Normally) | 15GB *shared* storage, 1 Custom Email Domain, 10 aliases for custom domain names, E2E Encryption | Well known brand with a reputation for respecting user privacy, frequently giving discounts on paid plans. However, their plans lack features and some users may reach their limit (i.e 10 email alias limit). Furthermore, they do have a solid ecosystem, but as mentioned before it is better to not put all your eggs in one basket. They also state that their servers are hosted in Germany/Switzerland too. |
| Zoho | £2.40 | 30GB Mail Storage, 30 Aliases for custom domain names | Cheap, and great price/value. Has the option for PGP Encryption. Where your email server is depends on where your account was registered, which can be both a positive and a negative. |
| FastMail | £4.50| 60GB Total Storage, 100 aliases for custom domain names, Encryption | On the more expensive side, but also has significantly more perks. It also (seemingly) has a more refined/polished workflow on their email applications. However, all backups for everybody are stored in the US as well as logs - which can be a deciding factor for some people choosing their email provider. BUT EU citizens have their data stored in Amsterdam, and users have the option to choose whether their mail server is in the US/EU. |

- I personally chose Mailbox.org because of their balance of pricing/value and their server locations. Proton was too pricey for what they were offering and also for the aforementioned reason (all eggs should not be in one basket); Zoho were okay, but details were hard to come across about their privacy mechanisms and Fastmail also cost too much.
